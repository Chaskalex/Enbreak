Writing a Grant Cover Letter

Writing Level B2 30 min

Imagine you have a research idea, a community initiative, or an academic project that could genuinely make a difference, but it needs funding. In many institutions and organisations, the first thing that decides whether your proposal gets read at all is a cover letter. One page. A few paragraphs. And yet those few paragraphs can open or close a door entirely.

In this activity, you will write a cover letter for an academic grant proposal. You will study the structure and conventions of formal academic correspondence, work with language for making a persuasive case, and practise the tone and register expected at B2 level in professional and academic contexts.

By the end of this activity, you will be able to write a structured, formal cover letter that presents an academic proposal clearly and persuasively, using appropriate conventions for B2-level academic writing.

Learning Objective

Write a formal cover letter for an academic grant proposal using appropriate register and structure.

Register Check

Decide whether the underlined language in each sentence is Formal and appropriate for a grant cover letter, or Too Informal for this context.

1. I am really passionate about this research area and have always wanted to study it.
Too informal. "Really passionate" is conversational. A more appropriate alternative: "I have a strong and sustained commitment to this research area."
2. The proposed research aims to identify the types of social media engagement most strongly associated with adverse mental health outcomes.
Formal. "Aims to" is a neutral, precise phrase widely used in academic writing. It states the purpose clearly without sounding conversational.
3. I think this project could be really useful for universities trying to help their students.
Too informal. "Really useful" is vague and conversational. A stronger alternative: "This research has the potential to make a significant contribution to student wellbeing policy."
4. I hold a first-class undergraduate degree in Psychology and have completed advanced coursework in research design.
Formal. "Hold a degree" and "have completed" are standard formal constructions for stating credentials. The sentence is precise and well-suited to academic correspondence.
5. Please have a look at my attached proposal and let me know if you need anything else.
Too informal. "Have a look at" and "let me know if you need anything else" are too casual. Better: "Please find enclosed my research proposal for your consideration."

The Selection Committee
Foundation for Academic Research Excellence
FARE Secretariat, London WC1E 7HX
Re: Application for the FARE Early-Career Research Grant 2025

Dear Members of the Selection Committee,

I am writing to apply for the FARE Early-Career Research Grant. I am currently enrolled as a second-year MRes student in Psychology at Hartwell University, where my research focuses on digital wellbeing and mental health in higher education.

Opening: Names the grant, states the degree and institution. Formal salutation used throughout.

My proposed research investigates the relationship between social media engagement patterns and levels of anxiety and depression among undergraduate students. The study will employ a mixed-methods approach, combining survey data with qualitative interviews, to identify which types of engagement are most strongly associated with adverse mental health outcomes.

Research Summary: Uses "proposed research investigates" and "aims to" constructions. Specific method (mixed-methods) adds credibility.

This research addresses a significant gap in institutional policy. Despite the growing prevalence of social media-related distress among students, universities currently lack evidence-based guidelines for responding to it. The findings will be made available to student services and policy teams at participating institutions.

Significance: Identifies a gap ("lack evidence-based guidelines"), names the beneficiaries, and states what the findings will produce.

I hold a first-class undergraduate degree in Psychology and have completed coursework in research methodology and data analysis. I am confident that my academic preparation positions me well to carry out this study.

Credentials: Uses "hold a degree" and "have completed" — standard formal constructions. Ends with a confident but measured statement of suitability.

Please find enclosed my full research proposal and curriculum vitae for your consideration. I would welcome the opportunity to discuss my application further and trust that you will give it your full consideration.

Closing: "Please find enclosed" is the standard formal phrase for enclosures. "I trust that" is more formal than "I hope that."
Yours sincerely,
Elena Voss
