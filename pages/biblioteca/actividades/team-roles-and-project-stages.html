Team Roles and Project Stages

Vocabulary Level B1+ 20 min

Whether you are working on a group assignment, managing a team at work, or planning a community event, projects tend to follow similar patterns. There is always a starting point, a set of tasks to complete, a group of people with different responsibilities, and a moment when everything comes together. The vocabulary you use to describe all of this clearly and precisely makes a real difference to how well your team communicates.

In this activity, you will study a set of words and phrases related to project work at B1+ level. You will learn vocabulary for the main stages of a project, common team roles and responsibilities, and expressions used when describing tasks, delegating work, and tracking progress.

By the end of this activity, you will be able to understand and use key vocabulary for project stages and team roles accurately in professional and academic contexts at B1+ level.

Learning Objective

Learn and use vocabulary for project stages, team roles and responsibilities in professional contexts.

Key Vocabulary

Study the two groups of words below. These are the key terms you will use throughout this activity. Read each definition and example sentence before moving on.

Project Stages

6 terms
brief n.
A document given at the start of a project that outlines the goals, requirements, and expectations.
The client sent us a brief before we started any design work.
scope n.
The defined range of tasks and goals that a project includes, and what it does not include.
We need to agree on the scope before the team can start work.
milestone n.
An important point in a project that marks the completion of a key stage or achievement.
Finishing the prototype was a major milestone for the whole team.
deadline n.
The date or time by which a specific piece of work must be completed.
We have a hard deadline of 30 June for the first progress report.
deliverable n.
A product, document, or result that must be produced and handed over as part of a project.
The first deliverable is a 10-page feasibility report for the client.
handover n.
The process of passing a completed task, project, or responsibility to another person or team.
The handover to the client's IT team went smoothly after testing.

Team Roles

5 terms
project manager n.
The person responsible for planning, leading, and monitoring the overall project.
The project manager set the timeline and assigned tasks to each team.
team lead n.
A person who manages a specific sub-group and its daily work within a larger project.
The design team lead reported progress at the weekly status meeting.
stakeholder n.
A person or group with a direct interest in the project's outcome or results.
All stakeholders were invited to the final presentation to approve the plan.
contractor n.
A person hired from outside the organisation to carry out a specific piece of work.
We hired a contractor to develop the mobile application for the launch.
end user n.
The person who will ultimately use the product or system once it is complete and live.
End users were consulted throughout the testing and feedback phase.

Who Said It?

Read what each person says about their role. Choose the correct team role from the options.

1. "I was brought in specifically to rebuild the company's website. Once I deliver it, the internal team takes over."
Contractor. This person was hired from outside specifically for one task and will not stay after the work is done. This is the defining feature of a contractor.
2. "I keep the full project on track: the timeline, the budget, and communication between all teams."
Project Manager. Managing the full timeline, budget, and cross-team communication describes the project manager's overall responsibility for the whole project.
3. "I manage the design sub-group's daily tasks and send progress reports to the project manager each Friday."
Team Lead. This person manages a specific sub-group (design) and reports to the project manager. They have responsibility for one part of the project, not the whole thing.
4. "My division funded this initiative. I expect regular updates and the right to approve any major decisions."
Stakeholder. This person has a financial and decision-making interest in the outcome. They funded the project and want influence over major choices — the classic stakeholder position.
5. "Once this new platform goes live, my team will be working with it every day. Our feedback during testing really matters."
End User. This person will use the product daily after launch. Their feedback is valuable not because they fund or manage the project, but because they are the final recipient of the result.
