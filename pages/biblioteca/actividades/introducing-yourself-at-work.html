Introducing Yourself at Work

Speaking Level A2 20 min

Think about the first time you start a new job, attend a professional event, or meet a colleague from another department. Before anything else happens, you have to do one thing: introduce yourself. In English, those first few sentences, the words you choose to describe who you are and what you do, shape the impression you leave from the very beginning.

In this activity, you will take part in a short role play in which you introduce yourself in a professional context. You will prepare key information about yourself and your role, and practise phrases for greeting someone, giving your name, explaining your job and department, and describing your daily routine at work.

By the end of this activity, you will be able to introduce yourself clearly and naturally in a professional or workplace context using simple, accurate phrases at A2 level.

Learning Objective

Introduce yourself and your role in a workplace context using A2-level language.

Key Phrases

Study these useful phrases before you speak. Each group covers one part of a workplace introduction. You do not need to memorise them — use them as a reference when you prepare your speaking task.

Greeting

  • "Hello, my name is [name]."
  • "Hi! I'm [name]."
  • "Nice to meet you."
  • "It's great to meet you."

Your Job Title

  • "I work as a [job title]."
  • "I'm a [job title]."
  • "My job is [title]."

Department or Company

  • "I work in the [department] department."
  • "I work at [company name]."
  • "I'm part of the [team name] team."

Your Location

  • "I'm based in [city / office]."
  • "I work from the [branch] office."
  • "I'm on the [floor] floor."

Your Main Tasks

  • "My main responsibility is [task]."
  • "In my job, I [verb phrase]."
  • "I mainly work on [task]."

How Long You've Been There

  • "I've been here for [time period]."
  • "I started [time] ago."
  • "I'm new — I only started last [week/month]."

Speaking Tips
  • Speak slowly and clearly. At A2 level, simple is better than fast.
  • You do not need to say everything at once. Start with your name and job, then add details.
  • Use short sentences: "I work as..." is clearer than "My position involves..."
  • Smile and make eye contact. A warm tone matters as much as the words.
Model Introduction
"Hi! My name is Alex and I'm new here — I only started on Monday. I work as a junior assistant in the HR department. My main job is helping with paperwork and organising meetings. I'm on the third floor, in the north wing. It's really nice to meet you!"
Greeting + name: "Hi! My name is Alex..." — simple and friendly.
How new: "I only started on Monday." — gives context naturally.
Job and department: "I work as a junior assistant in the HR department." — uses the key pattern.
Main task: "My main job is helping with paperwork..." — simple verb + -ing form.
Location: "I'm on the third floor, in the north wing." — brief and clear.
Closing: "It's really nice to meet you!" — warm and natural end.
