Formal or Informal?

Vocabulary Level C1 20 min

Have you ever re-read something you wrote and thought it sounded too casual? Or written something carefully formal only to realise it felt stiff and unnatural? In academic English, the words you choose signal far more than their meaning alone. Choosing between "find out" and "investigate", or between "show" and "demonstrate", tells your reader exactly what kind of writer you are.

In this activity, you will study a set of verbs and phrases that appear frequently in academic English. For each one, you will see both the formal and informal version, practise recognising register in context, and work on selecting the right choice for academic writing and presentations.

By the end of this activity, you will be able to identify when a word or phrase sounds too informal for an academic context and replace it with a more appropriate alternative, giving your writing and speech a more professional and precise register.

Learning Objective

Identify and use formal vocabulary and register in academic writing and speech.

Tip

At C1 level, register precision matters more than volume of vocabulary. Using one precise formal verb in the right place is more effective than using ten general ones. Keep these pairs in mind as you write and revise.

2

Identify the Register

Each sentence is taken from an academic text. The underlined word or phrase is either appropriate for academic writing or too informal. Decide which.

1. This section looks at the historical development of the theory.
Too informal. "Looks at" is conversational. In academic writing, use "examines" or "analyses" instead.
2. The findings indicate that the hypothesis was partially supported.
Formal. "Indicate" is a precise, neutral verb widely used for reporting research findings. It is fully appropriate at C1 academic level.
3. There are a lot of factors that contribute to student disengagement.
Too informal. "A lot of" is conversational. Use "numerous," "a range of," or "a significant number of" in academic writing.
4. The data demonstrate a statistically significant difference between the two groups.
Formal. "Demonstrate" is the standard academic verb for presenting empirical evidence. It is more precise than "show" and signals a C1 register.
5. The programme helped participants improve their confidence in public speaking.
Too informal. "Helped" is acceptable in everyday writing but too vague at C1 level. Use "enabled," "assisted," or "facilitated" for academic precision.
6. The study employed a longitudinal design spanning three academic years.
Formal. "Employed" is the appropriate academic verb for describing methodology. It is more precise and formal than "used."

Select the Best Replacement

Each sentence contains an informal word or phrase (shown in red). Choose the best formal replacement from the three options.

1. The researchers tried to find out whether early intervention reduces school dropout rates.
Investigated is the precise academic verb for systematic inquiry. "Looked into" is still informal, and "checked" lacks the academic specificity required at C1 level.
2. The proportion of students choosing STEM subjects got bigger substantially over the five-year period.
Increased is the standard formal verb for describing growth. "Went up" and "got larger" are too conversational for academic writing.
3. The discussion thinks about the broader social implications of the study's findings.
Considers is the appropriate academic verb for analytical reflection. "Looks at" is informal, and "talks about" is far too conversational.
4. The methodological framework used a three-stage model to assess participant progress.
Employed is the formal academic term for using a tool or method. "Made use of" is wordy and slightly informal; "had" is far too vague.
5. The review needs to acknowledge the limitations of the existing literature.
Is required to signals an academic expectation with the appropriate formal register. "Has to" and "wants to" are too conversational for formal academic writing.
